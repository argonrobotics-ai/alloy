# Alloy starter library: sources and how far to trust them

Layout (SPEC §3.2): `materials/<name>.yaml`, `pairs/<a>__<b>.yaml` (names sorted), `objects/<name>.yaml`,
`scenes/<name>.yaml` (demo scenes; `task_*.yaml` belong to WP13), `meshes/*.obj` (generated by
`python -m alloy.cards.meshgen library/meshes`, byte-for-byte reproducible, sha256 pinned in the cards; two exceptions:
`sample_tube_13x75.obj` and its `.hulls.obj` are OSS-9's own revolved round-bottom tube, made with alloy.create, licence
own, SCR OSS-9-5; `sponge_kitchen.obj` and its `.hulls.obj` are the 026 sponge scan of the YCB Object and Model Set (Calli
et al. 2015, 2017, ycbbenchmarks.com, licensed CC BY 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), sized to
90 x 60 x 25 mm and voxel-remeshed at 1.5 mm by OSS-9 with alloy.create, SCR OSS-9-5),
`labels.yaml` (expert routes, CARD-2), `catalog.yaml` (catalogue weights, CARD-10), `history.jsonl` (written by
`CardLibrary.put`). Card files are stored in canonical form (`dump_card`); edit them by hand freely, the loader
accepts unit strings and the shorthand forms described in `alloy/cards/__init__.py`.

## What `source` means here
- `measured`: a published measurement of this pair or material (e.g. the MIT push dataset, impact tables).
- `catalog`: a handbook, standard, datasheet or vendor value for the grade or product named in the note.
- `model`: a derived or engineering-judgement value; the note says how; `unc` is wide on purpose.
- `fit`: derived from a measured whole-object quantity (e.g. an effective density from a catalogue mass);
  such objects are excluded from CARD-10 because the comparison would be circular.
- `default`: nobody sourced it; it is listed in `ResolvedObject.default_leaves` and counts against coverage.

## References (short keys used in notes)
- Callister & Rethwisch, *Materials Science and Engineering: An Introduction*, 9th ed., Wiley 2014, App. B
  (density B.1, modulus B.2, Poisson ratio B.3).
- USDA Forest Products Laboratory, *Wood Handbook*, FPL-GTR-190 (2010), Table 5-3a (MOE, 12 % moisture) and ch. 4.
- Serway & Jewett, *Physics for Scientists and Engineers*, 9th ed., Table 5.1 (static/kinetic friction).
- Engineering ToolBox, friction coefficients table, engineeringtoolbox.com/friction-coefficients-d_778.html.
- Ashby, *Materials Selection in Mechanical Design*, 4th ed. (2011), App. A (foams, natural materials, tool steel).
- Gent (ed.), *Engineering with Rubber*, 3rd ed. (2012), ch. 1 (Young's modulus vs Shore A hardness).
- CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics, 97th ed. (2016) (water density, viscosity, surface tension).
- ISO 965-1 (metric thread tolerances), ISO 724 (basic dimensions), DIN 933/934/125-A (bolt, nut, washer),
  VDI 2230-1:2015 (friction classes).
- Yu, Bauza, Fazeli, Rodriguez, *More than a Million Ways to Be Pushed*, 2016, arXiv:1604.04038.
- Foerster, Louge, Chang & Allia, Phys. Fluids 6(3):1108 (1994) (glass-sphere impact friction and restitution).
- Klár et al., *Drucker-Prager Elastoplasticity for Sand Animation*, ACM TOG 35(4) 2016 (MPM sand parameters).
- Task-design parameters from an internal manipulation benchmark: object dimensions, catalogue masses and most
  pair friction values. Each carries its own primary citation (handbook, standard or data sheet) in its note.

## Verification level (read before using a value as a gate)
- Task-derived values and the catalogue masses in `catalog.yaml` were checked against their sources on
  2026-11-01.
- Handbook values (Callister, FPL, Serway, CRC, Ashby, Gent, data sheets) were entered by the WP1 builder from
  the named references without page-level re-checking in this session: **UNVERIFIED transcription**. Each such
  value carries an `unc` that spans the usual grade-to-grade spread, so the nominal can be wrong by its `unc`
  without changing a route. Spot-check before using one in a law threshold; WP11 fits replace them with `fit`.
- `model` values (cloth effective moduli, foam, dough, rice kernel modulus, cable composites, restitution
  defaults) are order-of-magnitude choices; calibrate them on a rig before trusting dynamics that depend on them.
- Known limitations: isotropic `Elastic` cannot carry a cable's measured GJ/EI = 1.64 (SCR-WP1-7); the bolt
  meshes model the thread as a pitch-diameter cylinder (CARD-10 shows +2.0 to +4.5 % mass); several household
  meshes (mug handle, jar wall, screwdriver grip) are simplified, and those objects are excluded from CARD-10.
